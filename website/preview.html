Information technology

IT tracks.

Each track is built around the tasks the role is actually hired to do.

Software and web development

Front end, back end and full stack work. You build and ship a working application with a database behind it, handle change requests, and learn to read a codebase you did not write, which is most of the job in reality.

Cyber security

Defensive and operational security practice. Monitoring, assessment, hardening, incident handling and reporting, taught with the documentation discipline that security roles are judged on.

Cloud and DevOps

Deployment, build pipelines, environments, monitoring and infrastructure. You take an application from a laptop to a running, observable service.

Testing and quality

Manual and automation testing against live builds. Writing test cases that hold up, logging defects that developers can act on, and retesting properly.

Data and analytics

Working with messy real datasets, building reports and dashboards, and explaining what the numbers mean to someone who is not technical.

Core engineering and non IT

Engineering tracks.

Core engineering graduates are underserved by most training institutes. We treat these tracks as seriously as the IT ones.

Electrical engineering

Design practice, load calculation, drawings, standards and documentation, plus what actually happens between a drawing and a site. You finish able to read, produce and defend real drawings.

Mechanical engineering

Design and drafting, tolerances, material selection, production documentation and the flow from concept to manufacturing. Built around the deliverables a junior engineer is handed in week one.

Design and drafting

Industry standard tools and the drawing conventions teams expect, with review cycles so you learn to take correction the way a design office runs it.

Operations and support roles

Process, coordination, reporting and stakeholder communication for business and support functions where the work is organisational rather than technical.

Common questions

Before you ask.

I have zero experience. Is that a problem?

No. That is exactly who this is built for. The whole reason we train on live projects is so that people with no experience finish with something real to talk about, and with a genuine idea of how the work is done.

Is this a batch or is it really one to one?

Sessions are one to one. Your trainer works with you alone, so the pace follows you. If something is not clear you can stop and go over it again without holding anyone up.

Do you only train IT people?

No. We run tracks for electrical, mechanical, design and other non IT roles as well, taught by trainers from those fields. Core engineering is not an afterthought here.

What happens after the training finishes?

We write your resume, prepare you for interviews and stay available for job support once you are placed. The training is the start of the relationship, not the end of it.

How long does a track take?

It depends on your domain, your starting point and how much time you can give each week. We work that out with you on the first call rather than quoting a fixed number that fits nobody.

Not sure which track fits you?

Tell us your background and the role you want. The first conversation is free and we will give you a straight answer.

Resume writing

Written for the screening software and for the human after it.

An applicant tracking system reads your resume before any recruiter does. If the structure confuses it, your application is gone before anyone knows you exist.

Clears the ATS filter

Clean structure, standard section headings, parseable formatting and the right keywords drawn from the actual job descriptions you are targeting.

Leads with outcomes

Responsibilities tell a recruiter nothing. We rewrite your experience around what you produced and what changed because you were there.

Targeted per role

One generic resume sent everywhere is why the calls stop. We build a base and show you exactly how to adjust it for each application.

Fresher friendly

No experience does not mean an empty page. Project work, training deliverables and academic work are positioned so they carry real weight.

Honest content only

We will not invent experience you do not have. We present what is genuinely yours in the strongest accurate way, which is what survives an interview.

Profile clean up

Your professional profile is checked alongside the resume, because recruiters look there immediately after and a mismatch costs you the call.

Interview preparation

The call comes. Now you have to hold the room.

A good resume only buys you the conversation. What loses most candidates is being unable to talk about their own work with any confidence.

We run mock interviews for your specific domain, work through the technical questions you will actually face, and rehearse how you explain your projects. Then we tell you plainly what was weak, so you can fix it before it counts.

Domain specific mocks

Explaining your own work
